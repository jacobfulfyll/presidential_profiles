How a speech becomes a label

The topic names were discovered from this corpus before the full annotation pass. The model did not receive a president's identity when making subjective judgments. Every label is keyed back to its paragraph, and a second model measures where the first one is uncertain.

First: what “AI-labeled” means here

There are two measurement layers on this website. The original issue charts use a seeded, deterministic CorEx topic model with hand-written anchor words. The newer layer uses a corpus-derived taxonomy and Claude to read paragraphs semantically. The old layer remains visible as an independent instrument; it is not relabeled as AI work after the fact. See the complete CorEx derivation and the side-by-side model comparison →

The unit is the paragraph. A speech can cover several subjects, switch from policy to ceremony, or name an adversary once. Paragraph-sized units keep those changes visible. All joins use (doc_name, para_idx), never row position.

How the 50 topics were arrived at

  1. Find the blind spots. The 15 anchored issues left 10,381 of 36,229 paragraphs without a match. Those missing paragraphs were deliberately oversampled.
  2. Let language cluster before naming it. Every paragraph was embedded with minishlab/potion-base-8M, normalized, and clustered with seeded MiniBatchKMeans at k=40. c-TF-IDF terms described what distinguished each cluster; the clusters were evidence, not final topics.
  3. Isolate eras. Nine 30-year samples were drawn with 80 paragraphs per era and roughly half from the old taxonomy's blind mass. Each era proposal saw only its own paragraphs, dominant clusters, and terms. The 1800s proposal could not borrow “health care” from a modern sample.
  4. Merge once, then freeze. Opus merged the era proposals into 17 domains and 50 period-aware topics, then produced a many-to-many crosswalk to the 16 existing issue pages.
  5. Test what was not used to build it. A held-out set of 200 paragraphs—100 from the former blind mass—was labeled against the frozen list. Coverage reached 100%, above the pre-registered 90% gate. The taxonomy was then frozen before the 36,229-paragraph pass.

The taxonomy-generation run used claude-opus-4-8 (+ claude-sonnet-5 for coverage), 22 API requests, and recorded $2.4404 in actual usage-derived cost.

What the annotator saw—and what it did not

The subjective paragraph pass received the paragraph text and its decade. President, party, and speech title were withheld. The decade allowed the rubric to judge combativeness against the speech's own era instead of silently imposing 2026 norms.

A second factual pass used the opposite policy: title, year, and the first five paragraphs were shown because those are direct evidence for speech type, audience, and medium. Masking a title like “Fourth Annual Message” would remove the best factual clue without reducing reputational bias.

The closed rubric

Structured output allowed only the following fields and closed enum values. Unknown topic strings could not be invented by the model.

topics

Zero or more names from the frozen 50-topic list. No label is forced when none fits; several may apply.

party_attack

True only when a partisan actor is identifiable and the text imputes fault, failure, or bad motive.

enemy_naming

True only when a specific person, group, nation, or institution is framed as an adversary or threat.

zero_sum

True only when the paragraph itself frames gains as requiring another side's loss—not merely when conflict is mentioned.

proposal

A concrete policy, program, law, appropriation, or course of action is urged.

values

Principles, identity, faith, gratitude, or civic virtue appear without a concrete policy ask.

mixed

A substantial concrete ask and a substantial values appeal both appear.

neither

Narrative, procedure, ceremony, factual reporting, or rebuttal that fits neither side.

entities

Specific actors are extracted with person/group/nation/institution/other and adversarial/favorable/neutral stance.

How every paragraph was labeled

Claude Sonnet 5 annotated all 36,229 paragraphs. Each request carried an exact count contract and the paragraph keys it had to return. Some long structured arrays stopped early, so incomplete speeches were never marked done: they were retried through seven rounds with smaller chunks until paragraph and speech coverage both reached 100%. Thirty-two speeches needed partial-speech chunk context; that amendment is recorded rather than hidden.

The final table contains 52,133 distinct paragraph-topic pairs, or 1.439 topics per paragraph. Labels are multi-label, so shares across topics do not sum to 100%. The denominator always includes the 404 paragraphs (1.12%) assigned no topic. Case variants are mapped to the canonical 50 names, and duplicate paragraph-topic pairs are removed before counting.

Quality checks and the second opinion

Coverage alone is not accuracy. A persisted, era-stratified sample of 266 speeches was read again by Claude Opus 4.8 using byte-identical prompts. The model was the only intended variable. Agreement is published field by field; no disagreement score is used to erase labels.

These bars are not one league table. Topic-set Jaccard, binary-label κ, exact match, and matched-name stance agreement answer different questions. The shared 0–1 axis makes their scale visible; each tooltip retains the metric type.

Those figures are reliability evidence, not human ground truth. Topic Jaccard is set overlap for a multi-label field; Cohen's kappa corrects categorical agreement for chance; exact match is used for the four-way proposal/values field. Entity-name matching is particularly strict because two models may choose different spans for the same actor; stance agreement is reported only on matched names.

How uncertainty reaches the charts

Trend bands resample whole speeches, not individual paragraphs, because paragraphs inside one speech are correlated. AI-topic trend bands combine that sampling interval with the measured half-gap between the primary and second-opinion labelers in the same reporting era. Thin periods are dotted; cells whose interval cannot be resolved receive a hollow ring. A point estimate may still be real while its uncertainty is unknown.

Where the labels appear

  • Dashboard: AI topic trends carry sampling and annotator-disagreement bands.
  • President profiles: top topics, distinctive topics, proposal/values mix, combat flags, genres, and named adversaries.
  • Comparison: the same rates are aligned across two or three presidents.
  • Issue pages: each broad legacy issue shows the finer AI topics in its crosswalk.
  • Explorer: all 50 AI topics can be overlaid against any word or phrase.

Limitations that remain visible

  • The Miller Center collection is curated and not exhaustive; this is presidential formal speech, not every rally, interview, tweet, or private conversation.
  • The taxonomy is frozen for comparability and therefore retains known gaps, including no standalone education topic and no modern monetary-policy topic.
  • Era tags reduce anachronism; they do not eliminate every historically implausible assignment.
  • Inter-model agreement is not human validation. Zero-sum framing is the least stable binary flag here (κ 0.49), so it should be read with more caution than speech type (κ 0.95).
  • President-level percentages are descriptive. Thin records are marked rather than promoted as precise estimates.

The complete frozen taxonomy

Foreign Relations & Diplomacypolicy · 8 topics

Conduct of relations with other nations: treaties, diplomacy, neutrality, alliances, hemispheric and great-power policy, foreign aid, and international organizations.

  • Neutral Rights & Maritime Depredations Protests against violations of American neutral commerce and the impressment of seamen by European belligerents during the era of the Napoleonic wars.
  • Treaties, Diplomacy & International Arbitration General conduct of foreign intercourse through treaties, conventions, boundary commissions, recognition of new states, and peaceful settlement of disputes by arbitration.
  • Relations with Spain, Mexico & Territorial Claims Boundary disputes, treaties, spoliation claims, and grievances with Spain and Mexico over Florida, Texas, and other borderlands.
  • Monroe Doctrine & Latin American Policy Assertion of hemispheric primacy and non-interference, sympathy for and relations with Latin American republics, and intervention in the Western Hemisphere.
  • Cold War & Great-Power Rivalry The global contest with Soviet and Chinese communism and later confrontation and negotiation with great-power rivals and rogue regimes over security and influence.
  • Nuclear Weapons & Arms Control The danger of nuclear war and fallout, superpower vulnerability, and efforts to limit or reduce nuclear arms through negotiation and treaties.
  • Middle East & Israel U.S. commitment to Israel's security, the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, and pursuit of Arab-Israeli peace and regional diplomacy.
  • World Order, Foreign Aid & Democracy Promotion The United Nations and international order, economic assistance to developing nations, and the conviction that America should spread liberty and democracy abroad.
War & Military Affairspolicy · 7 topics

The waging of wars and military campaigns, reports on operations, and the raising, equipping, and honoring of the armed forces and veterans.

  • Military Preparedness, Armed Forces & Veterans Organizing and equipping the militia, army, and navy, fortifications and armament, readiness in peacetime, and the care and honoring of veterans and service members.
  • Early Naval Wars: Barbary & the War of 1812 Naval operations against the Barbary powers and the conduct of the War of 1812 with Britain, including maritime independence and honorable peace.
  • Mexican War The military conflict with Mexico, its justification through claims and annexation, and the conduct and settlement of the war.
  • World War I Mobilization & War Aims America's entry into the European war, the draft and supply machinery, and the assertion of unselfish national ideals and purposes.
  • World War II Military Operations Reports on the progress of the Second World War, battles in the Pacific and Europe, casualties, and the strategy of defeating Germany and Japan.
  • Vietnam War Justification of U.S. involvement in South Vietnam, war conduct and troop levels, prisoners, and the search for a negotiated peace.
  • Iraq, Gulf Wars, the War on Terror & Interventions Confronting Iraqi aggression and terrorism, combating al Qaeda and weapons of mass destruction, and armed interventions and peacekeeping in trouble spots.
Money, Banking & Currencypolicy · 2 topics

Monetary policy, coinage and the mint, specie, paper currency, the gold standard, national and state banks, and financial crises.

  • Coinage, Currency & Specie The mint and coinage, gold and silver, specie versus paper money, the gold standard, and the free-silver and specie-resumption debates.
  • National Bank & Banking Crises Controversies over a national bank, deposit of public revenues, bank regulation, protection of deposits, and financial panics and revulsions.
Trade, Commerce & Tariffspolicy · 2 topics

Regulation of foreign and interstate commerce, tariffs, reciprocity, navigation laws, and control of corporate monopoly.

  • Tariffs, Reciprocity & Navigation Laws Import duties and protective tariffs, reciprocal trade and navigation treaties, tonnage, and disputes over trade practices with foreign rivals.
  • Trusts, Corporations & Antitrust Concern over concentrated corporate wealth and monopoly and proposals for federal supervision or regulation of interstate business.
Public Finance & Taxationpolicy · 2 topics

Government revenue, the public debt, appropriations, taxes, budgets, deficits, and federal spending.

  • Public Debt, Revenue & Treasury Finance Receipts and expenditures, reduction of the national debt, surplus revenue, and the general fiscal condition of the Treasury.
  • Taxes, Budget Deficits & Federal Spending Modern fiscal debates over tax cuts and incentives, budget deficits and inflation, spending controls, and balancing the budget.
Economy, Labor & Social Welfarepolicy · 5 topics

The general economic condition, employment, wages and labor relations, poverty and welfare, health and old-age security, and prosperity of ordinary Americans.

  • Great Depression Recovery Federal and voluntary responses to the economic collapse of the 1930s, including relief, public works, and recovery measures.
  • Labor, Wages & Working Conditions Relations between labor and capital, the right to organize, wages and labor legislation, child labor, and urban tenement and social-welfare reform.
  • Welfare, Poverty & Economic Opportunity Anti-poverty programs, welfare reform to reward work, the minimum wage, and helping families toward self-sufficiency.
  • Health Care, Medicare & Social Security Health insurance coverage and costs, Medicare and drug coverage, and the Social Security old-age system.
  • Prosperity, Jobs & the Middle Class Assessment of the nation's economic condition and appeals about jobs, growth, and security for working and middle-class families.
Agriculture, Land & Environmentpolicy · 3 topics

Farming and farm prices, disposal and settlement of public lands, and conservation of natural resources and the environment.

  • Public Lands & Homestead Settlement Survey, sale, pricing, preemption, and distribution of public lands, protection of settlers, and operation of the homestead system.
  • Agriculture & Farm Prices Development of farming, agricultural research and markets, farm credit and cooperatives, and the economic condition of farmers.
  • Conservation & the Environment Conservation of forests, water power, and public resources and later programs to clean the nation's air and water and preserve parklands.
Infrastructure & Internal Improvementspolicy · 2 topics

Federal support for roads, canals, harbors, energy, and other public works and large-scale national development projects.

  • Internal Improvements, Energy & Infrastructure Federal support for roads, canals, harbors, energy production and pipelines, and rebuilding the nation's infrastructure, including constitutional debate over such works.
  • Space Exploration The race to lead in space, the drive to reach the moon, and investment in rockets and satellites in competition with the Soviets.
Civil Rights & Immigrationpolicy · 5 topics

Slavery and emancipation, racial equality and voting rights, discrimination, and the admission and treatment of immigrants.

  • Slavery, Emancipation & Sectionalism Disputes over slavery in the territories and sectional compromise, and the emancipation, colonization, and new civil status of the freedmen.
  • Reconstruction & Southern Self-Government Pacification of the former Confederate states, restoration of local self-government, and protection of citizens' rights after the war.
  • Chinese Immigration & Exclusion The character of Chinese immigration and contract labor, registration and passport proposals, race prejudice, and mob violence against Chinese laborers.
  • Civil Rights, Voting Rights & Discrimination Federal action to end racial denial of the vote and secure equal rights, combat discrimination, and include the disabled and minorities in American life.
  • Immigration & Border Security Welcoming or restricting immigrants, border walls and enforcement, deportation and detention, and framing immigration as a security concern.

  • Indian Affairs, Removal & Allotment Relations with Indian tribes: frontier hostilities and treaties, efforts to 'civilize' them, removal beyond the states, reservation policy, and allotment of lands in severalty.
Law, Crime & Justicepolicy · 2 topics

Enforcement of federal law, suppression of insurrection, crime and courts, and morals legislation in the Territories.

  • Crime, Insurrection & Federal Law Enforcement Suppression of domestic insurrection and combinations against federal law, and the administration of justice through courts, penitentiaries, and law-enforcement agencies.
  • Polygamy in the Territories Legislation aimed at suppressing polygamy and bigamy in Utah and other Territories through marriage records, witness rules, and voting and office restrictions.
Constitutional Order & Governancepolicy · 5 topics

The constitutional structure of the government: federalism and union, executive power, civil-service reform, territorial organization and statehood, and the integrity of elections.

  • Constitutional Union & Federalism Reflections on the constitutional compact, the balance between state sovereignty and federal authority, secession, and preservation of the Union.
  • Civil Service Reform & the Merit System Competitive examinations, boards of examiners, merit-based appointment and promotion, and freeing offices from patronage.
  • Executive Power, Vetoes & the Courts Assertions of presidential constitutional duties, the veto and removal powers, interbranch relations, and reform of the federal judiciary.
  • Territorial Organization, Statehood & Insular Governance Establishing governments for new territories, fixing boundaries and admitting states, and administering overseas dependencies such as the Philippines, Cuba, and Puerto Rico.
  • Elections & Voting Integrity Federal supervision of elections and enforcement laws, disputed vote-counting, and modern controversies over voting rights and ballot integrity.
