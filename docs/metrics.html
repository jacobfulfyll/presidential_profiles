Attention, topics, and continuity

#

Effective topics

How many topics received meaningful attention?

A diversity score translated back into an intuitive number of equally attended topics.

Technical evidence
Unit
effective topics
Formula
exp(−Σ pᵢ ln pᵢ)
Example
Four equal topics score 4; one dominant topic scores close to 1.
Source
coverage_pressure.parquet
Status
confirmatory
Limitation
Depends on AI topic labels and paragraph boundaries.

Calculation

  1. Split words across paragraph topics
  2. Find each topic's speech share
  3. Calculate Shannon entropy
  4. Exponentiate the result
#

Topic depth

How long did a topic hold the floor?

The word length of a typical topic episode, giving long episodes proportionally more weight.

Technical evidence
Unit
equivalent words
Formula
Σ run_words² / Σ run_words
Example
A 50-word and 150-word episode produce (50²+150²)/(50+150)=125 words.
Source
coverage_pressure.parquet
Status
confirmatory
Limitation
Multi-label paragraphs divide their words equally across topics.

Calculation

  1. Find consecutive paragraphs carrying a topic
  2. Sum equivalent words in each run
  3. Weight longer runs by their length
#

Leading-topic share

How concentrated was the speech?

The fraction of topic exposure assigned to the speech's largest topic.

Technical evidence
Unit
share
Formula
max(topic words) / all topic words
Example
If the largest topic receives 300 of 1,000 equivalent words, the share is 30%.
Source
coverage_pressure.parquet
Status
secondary
Limitation
A low value can mean broad coverage or noisy labeling.

Calculation

  1. Find topic exposures
  2. Choose the largest
  3. Divide by all exposed words
#

Adjacent-paragraph similarity

Did neighboring paragraphs stay on the same subjects?

Average Jaccard overlap between the topic sets of neighboring paragraphs.

Technical evidence
Unit
Jaccard similarity
Formula
|A∩B| / |A∪B|
Example
Topics {A,B} beside {B,C} overlap by 1 of 3, or 0.33.
Source
coverage_pressure.parquet
Status
secondary
Limitation
Paragraph segmentation changes which pairs are neighbors.

Calculation

  1. Compare neighboring topic sets
  2. Divide overlap by union
  3. Average pairs
#

Share of paragraphs

How much of the record carries a label?

The number of labeled paragraphs divided by every paragraph in the selected record.

Technical evidence
Unit
percent of paragraphs
Formula
labeled paragraphs / eligible paragraphs
Example
Twenty labeled paragraphs among 100 total paragraphs is 20%.
Source
paragraph_annotations.parquet
Status
exploratory AI descriptive
Limitation
Paragraphs vary greatly in length, and AI labels can disagree.

Calculation

  1. Count paragraphs carrying the label
  2. Count every eligible paragraph
  3. Divide the labeled count by the total
#

Legacy issue attention

What share of a president's source-document paragraphs carries one displayed CorEx issue?

Boolean-positive paragraphs for the displayed CorEx issue divided by every corpus paragraph in speeches owned by that president.

Technical evidence
Unit
percent of source-document paragraphs
Formula
issue-positive source-document paragraphs / all source-document paragraphs
Example
Twenty positive paragraphs among 100 source-document paragraphs is 20% legacy issue attention.
Source
issues_president.parquet + paragraph_issues.parquet
Status
exploratory descriptive
Limitation
CorEx issues are multi-label and methodologically distinct from actual-speaker AI topics. Source-document ownership is not speaker attribution, and the measure is descriptive rather than causal.

Calculation

  1. Join paragraphs to speeches by document identity
  2. Group the joined paragraphs by source-document president owner
  3. Count Boolean-positive paragraphs for the displayed issue
  4. Divide by every source-document paragraph owned by that president

lifecycle class
Formula
thresholded attention timeline
Example
A topic absent for 35 years and later returning is marked revived.
Source
topic_lifecycles.parquet
Status
exploratory
Limitation
Boundary years depend on corpus coverage and the declared threshold.

Calculation

  1. Build a smoothed attention curve
  2. Apply the substantive threshold
  3. Find first, last, and long gaps
  4. Assign born/died/persistent/revived
#

Actual-speaker topic presence

What share of one president's eligible speaker-audited paragraph record carries an AI-assigned topic?

Distinct eligible paragraphs actually attributed to the president and carrying the canonical topic, divided by every eligible paragraph attributed to that president in the complete corpus or one accepted Story era. Topic-free eligible paragraphs remain in the denominator.

Technical evidence
Unit
speaker-audited paragraph share
Formula
topic paragraphs attributed to president / all eligible paragraphs attributed to president in scope
Example
Twenty topic-bearing paragraphs among 100 eligible paragraphs actually attributed to the president is a 20% speaker-paragraph share.
Source
speaker_topic_network/edges_v1.parquet
Status
exploratory AI descriptive
Limitation
Multi-label shares are non-additive and may sum above 100%. AI topic presence is descriptive and non-causal; it is not confidence, intent, importance, influence, success, or representation of the public or presidency as a whole. Supported edges additionally require five topic paragraphs and two topic-bearing appearances.

Calculation

  1. Use only actual-president paragraphs marked eligible by the accepted speaker foundation
  2. Count each normalized topic once per paragraph and derive each Level 1 parent once
  3. Keep topic-free eligible paragraphs in the president denominator
  4. Compute corpus-wide and accepted Story-era scopes without recomputing eras
  5. Label presidents with 1–4 eligible actual-speaker appearances thin and require five appearances for supported status
#

Actual-speaker topic contribution

What share of a scope's eligible paragraph evidence assigned to a topic comes from one actual president speaker?

Distinct eligible topic-bearing paragraphs actually attributed to the president, divided by all eligible paragraphs carrying that canonical topic across actual president speakers in the complete corpus or one accepted Story era.

Technical evidence
Unit
share of eligible topic-bearing paragraphs
Formula
topic paragraphs attributed to president / all eligible paragraphs carrying topic in scope
Example
If 30 of a scope's 120 eligible paragraphs carrying a topic are actually attributed to one president, the topic-contribution share is 25%.
Source
speaker_topic_network/edges_v1.parquet
Status
exploratory AI descriptive
Limitation
Multi-label topic shares are non-additive and may sum above 100%; topic-free paragraphs create no topic contribution but remain in scope corpus denominators. The measure is descriptive and non-causal, not confidence, intent, importance, influence, success, or representation of the public or presidency as a whole.

Calculation

  1. Use the accepted actual-speaker population and keyed topic memberships
  2. Count every distinct normalized topic presence fully in multi-label paragraphs
  3. Divide the president's topic paragraphs by all scope paragraphs carrying that topic
  4. Retain explicit paragraph and actual-speaker appearance counts
  5. Mark 1–4-appearance presidents and under-threshold edges as thin

Language, form, and temporal framing

#

Rate per 10,000 words

How often does wording appear after allowing for length?

A count divided by all words and scaled to a same-sized 10,000-word container.

Technical evidence
Unit
mentions per 10,000 words
Formula
count / words × 10,000
Example
Five mentions in 10,000 words equals 5 per 10,000.
Source
speech_markers.parquet
Status
descriptive
Limitation
Words can carry different meanings in context.

Calculation

  1. Count included words
  2. Count all words
  3. Divide and multiply by 10,000
#

Centered five-year lexical rate

How often does one exact form or audited word family appear around each corpus year?

Exact or grouped matches and all indexed source-document words are summed from center year minus two through center year plus two; the match total is divided by the word total and scaled to 10,000 only when the window exceeds 20,000 indexed words.

Technical evidence
Unit
uses per 10,000 indexed words
Formula
Σ matches(center−2…center+2) / Σ indexed words(center−2…center+2) × 10,000
Example
Forty matches among 80,000 indexed words in a centered five-year window equals 5 uses per 10,000 words.
Source
speeches.parquet + word_families.json
Status
descriptive
Limitation
A match does not establish meaning, intent, endorsement, or context. Grouped forms follow an audited deterministic algorithm and do not imply universal linguistic equivalence; sparse windows are unknown rather than zero.

Calculation

  1. Tokenize every complete source-document transcript with the Explore word tokenizer
  2. For grouped series, replace forms only through the frozen audited word-family map
  3. Sum matches and indexed words over the five calendar years centered on the displayed year
  4. Publish only when the window contains more than 20,000 indexed words
  5. Divide matches by words and multiply by 10,000
#

Flesch–Kincaid grade level

How difficult is the sentence structure and vocabulary?

An estimate of the U.S. school grade needed to read a passage, based on sentence length and syllables per word.

Technical evidence
Unit
U.S. grade level
Formula
0.39(words/sentences) + 11.8(syllables/words) − 15.59
Example
A score of 8 suggests eighth-grade sentence and word complexity; it does not mean only eighth graders can understand it.
Source
speech_stats.parquet
Status
descriptive
Limitation
It estimates surface difficulty, not accuracy, eloquence, intelligence, or the complexity of the ideas. Names, quotations, and formulaic legal language can move the score.

Calculation

  1. Count words and sentences
  2. Estimate syllables in each word
  3. Calculate average sentence length and syllables per word
  4. Combine them on the Flesch–Kincaid grade scale

Technical evidence

share of corpus speeches

Status
descriptive
Limitation

A speech can contain both families and therefore score highly on both.

register/trends.parquet

#

Temporal appeals by president and over time

How often does presidential wording invoke tomorrow or recovery of yesterday?

Future and nostalgia matches per 10,000 marker words position each president and form two separate trailing four-year rolling averages of annual rates.

Technical evidence
Unit
matches per 10,000 marker words
Formula
future rate = future/words × 10,000; nostalgia rate = nostalgia/words × 10,000
Example
A president at 8 future and 5 nostalgia matches per 10,000 words sits at (8, 5); a timeline point averages the four annual rates ending in its displayed year.
Source
speech_markers.parquet + speeches.parquet
Status
descriptive
Limitation
Each year receives equal weight in the rolling average even when its speech volume differs. Dictionary matches do not establish intent, feasibility, or historical accuracy; genre, quotations, other speakers, and uneven coverage can change the rates.

Calculation

  1. Pool every available corpus speech assigned to the president
  2. Count future and nostalgia families and divide each by marker words
  3. For the timeline, calculate each annual rate and average four consecutive years
  4. Require coverage in all four years and at least 10,000 total window words

Hope divided by doom

How much hope vocabulary appears for each doom term in one supported time window?

The aggregate NRC hope count divided by the aggregate doom-family count in a centered five-year window.

Technical evidence
Unit
hope matches per doom match
Formula
Σ NRC hope matches / Σ doom matches
Example
A value of 80 means eighty NRC hope matches for each doom-family match in that window.
Source
speech_markers.parquet
Status
descriptive
Limitation
The two dictionaries differ greatly in breadth. A ratio can rise when hope increases or doom falls, so absolute rates remain in tooltips.

Calculation

  1. Sum NRC hope, doom, and words within the window
  2. Require at least 20,000 words
  3. Suppress a zero doom denominator
  4. Divide aggregate counts

Coarse part-of-speech share

Technical evidence
Unit

Formula

Example

Source

People, institutions, conflict, and networks

#

Era-distinctive reference

Which normalized names are unusually concentrated in one Story era?

Positive Jeffreys-smoothed paragraph log odds compare one era with the other eight on eligible speaker-audited paragraphs.

Technical evidence
Unit
speaker-audited paragraph log odds
Formula
log((era name paragraphs + 0.5)/(era other paragraphs + 0.5)) − log((other-era name paragraphs + 0.5)/(other-era other paragraphs + 0.5))
Example
A name supported by six paragraphs in an era and none elsewhere can rank above a more common name that is also widespread across other eras.
Source
reference_entities/era_distinctive_v1.parquet + speaker_views/paragraph_view_v1.parquet
Status
exploratory descriptive
Limitation
AI + NER and AI only describe source agreement, not historical validation or confidence. Distinctive does not mean important, representative, prevalent, endorsed, or historically significant.

Calculation

  1. Count each normalized entity once per eligible paragraph
  2. Keep names supported by at least five paragraphs and two source documents
  3. Apply a 0.5 Jeffreys prior to present and absent counts in the era and comparison corpus
  4. Rank positive log odds by score, era paragraphs, source documents, then normalized name
#

Reference-type paragraph presence

What kinds of named actors and places enter an era's presidential record?

Distinct eligible paragraphs actually attributed to a president and carrying at least one primary-AI-backed entity of the named type, divided by every eligible actual-president paragraph in the Story era.

Technical evidence
Unit
share of speaker-audited paragraphs
Formula
distinct paragraphs carrying entity type / all eligible actual-president paragraphs
Example
If 100 of 500 eligible actual-president paragraphs name at least one person, person presence is 20%.
Source
reference_entities/entity_mentions_v1.parquet + speaker_views/paragraph_view_v1.parquet
Status
exploratory descriptive
Limitation
Entity types come from the primary AI and can overlap within a paragraph, so the four shares are non-additive. NER source agreement does not establish confidence, stance, or historical importance.

Calculation

  1. Use every eligible actual-president paragraph in the era as the denominator
  2. Keep primary-AI-backed people, institutions, groups, and nations or places
  3. Count a paragraph once per entity type even when it names several entities of that type
  4. Divide each type's distinct paragraph count by the complete era denominator

#

Cross-model reproducibility by Story era

Field-appropriate agreement is recomputed within the nine governed chronological Story eras, with paragraph and speech support retained.

Technical evidence
Unit
agreement, overlap, or exact-match scale by Story era
Formula
agreement(field | governed Story era)
Example

Source
agreement_v1.parquet + data/quality/agreement_by_field_and_era.csv
Status
cross-model reproducibility
Limitation

  4. Cluster resampling by speech and report support

Technical evidence
Unit
percent of entity instances or paragraph-level Jaccard
Formula
name match = matched instances / union instances; stance = matching stance / matched instances
Example
Both-empty paragraphs increase all-pair Jaccard but do not contribute a detected name to the stance stage.
Source
data/quality/entity_name_agreement.csv + data/quality/entity_stance_matches.csv
Status
cross-model reproducibility
Limitation
Case normalization is not complete historical alias resolution, and model-to-model reproducibility is not human validity.

Calculation

  2. Pair detections by paragraph and name
  3. Count matched detections among the detection union
  4. Evaluate stance only on matches and publish both Jaccard denominators

Exact agreement

How often are two normalized judgments identical?

The share of paired records whose complete normalized value is identical across the two model passes.

Technical evidence
Unit
percent of pairs
Formula
identical normalized pairs / eligible completed pairs
Example
For a multi-label field, {A, B} and {B, A} match after normalization, while {A} and {A, B} do not.
Source
agreement_v1.parquet
Status
cross-model reproducibility
Limitation
Exact agreement can be dominated by common negatives and does not correct for chance or partial set overlap.

Calculation

  1. Normalize both values
  2. Compare each keyed pair
  3. Count identical pairs
  4. Divide by every eligible completed pair

Intersection size divided by union size, with the treatment of two empty sets declared explicitly.

Technical evidence

Formula

  2. Count their intersection
  3. Count their union
  4. Divide and state whether both-empty pairs are included

Annual transition abruptness

Where do adjacent historical windows differ most across the governed feature system?

The Euclidean magnitude of right-minus-left standardized axis changes is ranked separately for symmetric four- and eight-year windows, then their within-window percentiles are averaged.

Technical evidence
Unit
0–1 consensus abruptness percentile
Formula
consensus = (percentile(rank₄) + percentile(rank₈)) / 2
Example
Several adjacent high-scoring years form one annual transition mountain rather than several independent boundaries.
Source
era_boundaries/consensus_scores.parquet + era_boundaries/sliding_scores.parquet
Status
derived sensitivity diagnostic
Limitation
Abruptness diagnoses change but cannot choose a historically meaningful boundary by itself; overlapping windows are dependent.

Calculation

  1. Build governed feature vectors on both sides of every eligible year
  2. Scale each right-minus-left axis difference by that axis's population standard deviation across governed 8-year-bin fingerprints
  3. Measure the right-minus-left vector magnitude
  4. Combine four- and eight-year percentile ranks

Era segmentation sensitivity

Which proposed boundaries persist when reasonable modeling choices change?

A condition matrix records the dynamic-programming partition starts and compares each partition with the primary nine-segment condition across cluster counts, minimum lengths, grid offsets, weighting, terminal coverage, feature omissions, and common genre.

Technical evidence
Unit
boundary support, objective, and adjusted Rand similarity
Formula
argmin partition Σ within-segment squared distance
Example
A boundary supported under most comparable conditions is robust within this specification space, not historically inevitable.
Source
era_boundaries/sensitivity.parquet + era_boundaries/meta.json
Status
derived sensitivity analysis
Limitation
Results depend on the declared feature system and condition grid; the incomplete terminal cycle is explicitly right-censored.

Calculation

  1. Build four-year cycle fingerprints
  2. Standardize the retained feature axes
  3. Minimize within-segment squared distance under each declared condition
  4. Compare starts, support, objective, and partition similarity

Uncertainty envelope

How much uncertainty comes from sampling and AI disagreement?

The displayed interval width is the upper bound minus the lower bound in percentage points; a combined interval expands sampling variation by measured annotator disagreement.

Technical evidence
Unit
percentage-point interval width (source bounds stored as 0–1 shares)
Formula
displayed width = (upper share − lower share) × 100 percentage points
Example
A sampling-only 4–8% range is 4 percentage points wide; a 3–10% combined range is 7 points wide.
Source
data/quality/uncertainty_decomposition.csv (derived from bands.parquet)
Status
quality-aware descriptive
Limitation
Disagreement measured on a sample is assumed to transfer to the corpus.

Calculation

  1. Bootstrap speeches with source bounds stored as 0–1 shares
  2. Measure paired-model disagreement where applicable
  3. Combine the two components
  4. Subtract lower from upper and multiply by 100 for the displayed width

No-change surprise rate

How surprising is this under a no-change model?

The share of no-change repetitions at least as extreme as the observed result.

Technical evidence
Unit
percent
Formula
(extreme draws + 1) / (draws + 1)
Example
4.2% means about four of 100 no-change repetitions were this extreme—not a 4.2% chance the finding is random.
Source
inference_receipts.parquet
Status
frequentist
Limitation
It is not the probability that a finding is random, real, or important.

Calculation

  1. Specify the no-change model
  2. Repeat the test
  3. Count equally or more extreme results

Limitation
