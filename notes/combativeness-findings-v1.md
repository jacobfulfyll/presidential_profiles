# Combativeness over time (v1)

**Question.** Is right now uniquely combative, or does it just feel that way from inside?

**Answer, in one line.** On the one genre present in every era — the annual message / State of
the Union — the present era attacks the opposing party at **4.8× the Civil War rate**
(95% CI **2.3–14.3×**) and at **≥3.9× every other era with a computable interval**
(3.1× the n=3 War & New Deal cell, which has no interval), and that result survives every genre
control we can apply. Naming enemies and zero-sum framing are elevated too, but they are *not*
unprecedented: the present era is statistically indistinguishable from the founding era on both
(enemy-naming ratio 1.09, CI 0.79–1.61; zero-sum ratio 1.76, CI 0.95–4.88 — both intervals
contain 1).

**On precision.** Ratios are point estimates of a quantity the corpus constrains only loosely.
"4.8×" is the centre of a wide interval; what the data establishes firmly is *substantially
higher*, not 4.8 to two significant figures. Every ratio quoted below carries its bootstrap
interval for that reason.

Generated by `src/presidential_profiles/combat.py`; tables under `data/combat/`.
Zero API calls — pure local compute over the frozen annotation parquets.

## 1. The headline: SOTU-only, the apples-to-apples series

The corpus admits more informal genres over time (annual messages are 55–76% of 19th-century
paragraphs but 21% of the present era's), so a raw all-genre rise is confounded with the *corpus*
changing rather than *presidents* changing. The annual message is the only genre present in every
era. This is the co-primary series, not a control.

Paragraph-level rates, 95% speech-clustered bootstrap CIs:

| Era | party_attack | enemy_naming | zero_sum | speeches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The founding (1789–1815) | 0.002 [0.000, 0.006] | 0.262 [0.184, 0.345] | 0.084 [0.030, 0.148] | 27 |
| Expansion (1816–1849) | 0.014 [0.003, 0.029] | 0.189 [0.141, 0.241] | 0.050 [0.028, 0.079] | 34 |
| Civil War & Recon. (1850–1877) | 0.030 [0.010, 0.054] | 0.152 [0.117, 0.190] | 0.041 [0.026, 0.057] | 28 |
| The Gilded Age (1878–1900) | 0.010 [0.005, 0.017] | 0.082 [0.066, 0.103] | 0.025 [0.015, 0.036] | 23 |
| Progressives & Depression (1901–1932) | 0.006 [0.001, 0.015] | 0.061 [0.040, 0.086] | 0.016 [0.009, 0.025] | 32 |
| War & New Deal (1933–1945) | 0.046 — *no CI* | 0.379 — *no CI* | 0.299 — *no CI* | **3** |
| The Cold War (1946–1988) | 0.017 [0.007, 0.030] | 0.101 [0.081, 0.122] | 0.051 [0.035, 0.069] | 34 |
| Post-Cold War (1989–2016) | 0.036 [0.022, 0.052] | 0.162 [0.120, 0.204] | 0.064 [0.039, 0.090] | 27 |
| **The present era (2017–2026)** | **0.142 [0.095, 0.184]** | **0.286 [0.242, 0.328]** | **0.148 [0.118, 0.181]** | 10 |

**The three flags tell three different stories. That is why there is no composite index.**

- **`party_attack` — genuinely unprecedented.** 0.142 vs 0.030 for the Civil War era
  (**4.8×**, CI 2.3–14.3) and vs 0.036 for Post-Cold War, the closest era with a computable
  interval (**3.9×**, CI 2.3–6.9). The present-era interval [0.095, 0.184] does not overlap the
  interval of any era that has one — the highest other upper bound is the Civil War's 0.054.
  (War & New Deal is the one era with no interval to compare against; its point ratio is 3.1×.)
  One in seven paragraphs of a modern annual message attacks the opposing party by name.
- **`enemy_naming` — elevated, but not unprecedented.** 0.286 is the highest of any era with a
  computable interval; only the n=3 War & New Deal cell (0.379, no interval) is nominally higher.
  But the founding era's 0.262 [0.184, 0.345] overlaps it comfortably — the ratio is **1.09, CI
  0.79–1.61**, an interval containing 1. Madison naming Britain in 1813 and Trump naming China in
  2026 are, on this measure, statistically the same behaviour.
- **`zero_sum` — high, but not separable from the founding.** 0.148 vs 0.041 for the Civil War
  (**3.7×**, CI 2.4–5.8) is a real gap. Against the founding (0.084) it is **1.76×, CI
  0.95–4.88** — the interval contains 1, so the present era is *not* statistically distinguishable
  from the founding on zero-sum framing. It is the modern high (Cold War 0.051, Post-Cold War
  0.064); it is not an all-time high the data can defend.

So: **the "uniquely combative" intuition is right about partisan attack and wrong about enemies.**
Presidents have always named enemies. What is new is naming the *other party* as the enemy.

## 2. Raw vs within-genre: the divergence, stated explicitly

This is the confound that cuts against the hypothesis, so it gets its own section. Three
treatments, present era:

| Treatment | party_attack | enemy_naming | zero_sum |
|---|---|---|---|
| **raw** (all genres) | 0.152 | 0.337 | 0.161 |
| **sotu_only** (co-primary) | 0.142 | 0.286 | 0.148 |
| **genre_standardized** | 0.141 | 0.314 | 0.158 |

**The three treatments agree to within 1.1 percentage points on `party_attack`.** The present
era's partisan-attack result is *not* a genre artifact. If the corpus's drift toward informal
genres were manufacturing the finding, SOTU-only would collapse it. It does not.

For `enemy_naming` the divergence is real but modest: raw 0.337 → SOTU-only 0.286, a 15%
haircut. Some of the apparent rise in enemy-naming *is* the corpus admitting press conferences
and campaign remarks.

Where genre *does* rewrite the story is the 1930s — see §3.

`genre_standardized` reweights each era's genre mix to a fixed reference (the pooled corpus
paragraph share by speech type), using only (era, genre) cells with ≥3 speeches, and reports
`ref_weight_covered` — the share of the reference distribution each era actually spans. Coverage
runs 0.58 (Expansion, Gilded Age) to 1.00 (Progressives). Read the standardized column with that
number in hand.

## 3. The three peaks: 1860s vs 1930s vs 2020s

The question named three *decades*; the reporting axis is `trends.ERAS`. The two disagree in
ways that matter, so the peaks get their own decade-level table (`data/combat/peak_decades.parquet`):

| Decade | Treatment | party_attack | enemy_naming | zero_sum | speeches |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1860s** | raw | 0.090 | 0.226 | 0.052 | 55 |
| | SOTU-only | 0.047 | 0.159 | 0.047 | 10 |
| | standardized ‡ | 0.038 | 0.218 | 0.082 | 53 |
| **1930s** | raw | **0.181** | 0.239 | 0.066 | 40 |
| | SOTU-only † | **0.000** *(no CI, n=3)* | 0.019 *(n=3)* | 0.000 *(n=3)* | **3** |
| | standardized ‡ | 0.079 | 0.120 | 0.032 | 35 |
| **2020s** | raw | 0.159 | 0.339 | 0.153 | 61 |
| | SOTU-only | 0.151 | 0.287 | 0.141 | 7 |
| | standardized | 0.147 | 0.328 | 0.156 | 56 |

**‡ The 1860s and 1930s standardized cells carry NO interval.** Both lean heavily on 3-speech
strata (1860s 31% of weight, 1930s 55% — the 1930s figure is the same 3 Hoover annual messages
the row above suppresses), so the thin-stratum rule refuses them an interval (§8.6). The point
estimates stand; the precision does not. Only the 2020s standardized row (thin-stratum weight
0.024) carries a published interval: **0.147 [0.107, 0.186]** on `party_attack`.

**‡‡ The two decades are standardized to DIFFERENT realized mixes, and it cuts against the
headline.** Standardization only reweights genres an era actually has at ≥3 speeches. The 2020s
has just **2** campaign speeches, so `campaign_or_debate` is **dropped** from its reweight — while
the 1930s has 6 and **keeps** it at 11.5% of weight, at a 0.490 party-attack rate. The most
combative genre is therefore excluded from one side of the comparison and retained on the other.
**That is conservative for the finding**: including the 2020s campaign stratum (rate 0.432) would
push the 2020s standardized figure *up*, widening the gap the report claims, not narrowing it.
Coverage differs correspondingly — 1930s 0.904 vs 2020s 0.733.

**† The 1930s SOTU cell is HOOVER, not FDR.** All three annual messages in that decade are
Herbert Hoover's (1930, 1931, 1932). The corpus carries none of FDR's 1934–1940 annual messages
(§8.1). The bold **0.000** therefore says "Hoover's three annual messages contain no scored
partisan attack" — it emphatically does *not* say FDR's annual messages had none, and it is an
n=3 point with no interval either way.

**The 1930s is the genre story, and it is dramatic.** On the raw all-genre series the 1930s
*out-attacks the 2020s* (0.181 vs 0.159) — the single most combative decade in the corpus on
`party_attack`. But that peak is almost entirely **campaign oratory**: `campaign_or_debate` holds
just **6 speeches** in the decade, yet they run a **0.490** party-attack rate and supply
**140 of the decade's 164 flagged paragraphs — 85%**. Four of the six are Hoover's 1932 campaign
(Madison Square Garden, Indianapolis, St Paul, and the nomination acceptance); the other two are
FDR's 1936 convention and Madison Square Garden speeches. The 1930s `party_attack` peak is, to a
first approximation, **two election campaigns**.

Those speeches occupy **31.6%** of the decade's paragraphs against **10.4%** in the reference
genre mix. Standardization down-weights them to the reference share, and that single correction is
what drives the drop.

The decade's other novel genre — **14 fireside chats**, among the 15 broadcast
`public_remarks_or_address` speeches that make up 35% of the decade's paragraphs — is
comparatively mild on this flag at **0.059**, contributing just **19 of the 164** flagged
paragraphs (12%). FDR's Fireside Chat 13 is literally titled *"On Purging
the Democratic Party"* and is the era's top-ranked `party_attack` exemplar (§7), but it is
vivid rather than representative: radio is where the 1930s *talked*, not where it *attacked*.

Hold the genre mix fixed and the 1930s falls by more than half (0.181 → 0.079) while the 2020s
barely moves (0.159 → 0.147) — but see **‡‡** above: the two decades are standardized to
different realized mixes, in the direction that *understates* the gap.
**Campaign season, not the presidency, is most of the 1930s peak.**
That is the "corpus changing, not presidents changing" outcome the plan warned about — and it
lands on the 1930s, not on the present.

The 1860s is *lower than the 2020s* on `party_attack` under all three treatments, and lower than
the 1930s under raw (0.090 vs 0.181) and standardized (0.038 vs 0.079). Under SOTU-only the
ordering flips — the 1860s (0.047) is *higher* than the 1930s (0.000) — but that comparison is
between two n=3-to-10 cells, one of which carries no interval at all, so it will not bear weight.
On `enemy_naming` the 1860s standardized rate (0.218) sits well below the 2020s (0.328) but well
above the 1930s (0.120) — see §5 for why the era-level Civil War number understates it.

## 4. Is it just one president?

The present era's SOTU cell is 10 speeches: 6 Trump, 4 Biden. It is a fair question whether the
finding is one man.

| President | speeches | party_attack | enemy_naming | zero_sum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Donald Trump | 6 | 0.170 | 0.293 | 0.154 |
| Joe Biden | 4 | 0.104 | 0.276 | 0.139 |

**No.** Biden's annual messages alone run 0.104 on `party_attack` — still **3.5×** the Civil War
era and **2.9×** the Post-Cold War era. Those two are **point estimates with no interval**: at 4
and 6 speeches, both per-president sub-cells fall below this report's own 5-cluster CI floor
(§8.1), so they are offered as a robustness check on *direction*, not as measurements. And
per-speech, the *lowest* present-era annual message
(Biden 2022, 0.043) exceeds the era mean of every prior era with a computable interval — the
highest of which is Post-Cold War at 0.036. (The one prior cell it does not clear is War & New
Deal at 0.046, which is the n=3 suppressed cell and not an estimate.) The second-most partisan
single annual message in the present era is Biden's 2024 (0.213), ahead of five of Trump's six.

**How far that goes.** It rules out the simplest alternative — that one unusually combative
president accounts for the era. It does **not** establish "a regime shift, not a personality":
both sub-cells sit below this report's own CI floor (4 and 6 speeches), the era is 10 speeches
across 2 presidents, and a third administration could move it (§8.10). The supportable claim is
that **the present era's elevation is not attributable to a single president**, and that both
presidents in it attack at rates no prior era's average approaches.

One caution on the per-speech floor: Biden 2022 at 0.043 clears every prior era's *mean*, but it
sits **inside** the Post-Cold War era's interval [0.022, 0.052]. The floor claim is a
point-to-point comparison, not a separation.

Per-president figures are in `data/combat/by_president.parquet`.

## 5. Bail condition #1 (Civil War enemy-naming): adjudicated — **does not fire**

> *Plan bail condition: "The Civil War era should show elevated enemy-naming; if it does not, the
> annotation or the era anchoring is broken."*

At face value the condition looks tripped: Civil War `enemy_naming` is 0.190 all-genre / 0.152
SOTU — *below* the founding (0.258) and Expansion (0.203). We did not rubber-stamp it and we did
not wave it away. Four independent lines of evidence say the flag is behaving correctly and the
**bail condition's premise was mis-specified**, not the annotation. They are **§5.1, §5.2, §5.3
and §5.5**. A candidate fifth line — §5.4, genre standardization — was examined and
**withdrawn**: it does not survive its own uncertainty. It is kept below rather than deleted,
because a line of evidence that failed on inspection is part of the record, and the subsection
numbering is left unshifted so earlier cross-references still resolve.

### 5.1 `enemy_naming` is composition-sensitive, and a civil war inverts the composition

Decomposing the flag by *who* gets named, using the independent `paragraph_entities.type` field
(nation = foreign adversary; person/group/institution = domestic).

**Read the two components carefully — they are not a partition.** "…foreign component" is the
rate of paragraphs naming *any* foreign adversary (they may name domestic ones too);
"…domestic-only" is *exclusive* — it requires no foreign adversary at all. So the two do not sum
to the total, and paragraphs naming both are counted only in the foreign column. This asymmetry
is deliberately conservative for the argument below: it assigns every mixed paragraph *away* from
the domestic column that the Civil War case depends on. Entities typed `other` fall in neither.

| Era | enemy_naming | …foreign component | …domestic-only component |
|---|---|---|---|
| The founding | 0.258 | **0.153** | 0.097 |
| Expansion | 0.203 | 0.099 | 0.100 |
| **Civil War & Recon.** | 0.190 | **0.040** | **0.143** |
| The Gilded Age | 0.108 | 0.038 | 0.064 |
| Progressives & Depression | 0.107 | 0.020 | 0.083 |
| War & New Deal | 0.358 | 0.163 | 0.174 |
| The Cold War | 0.236 | 0.086 | 0.131 |
| Post-Cold War | 0.210 | 0.043 | 0.157 |
| The present era | 0.337 | 0.087 | 0.233 |

**The Civil War era's domestic enemy-naming (0.143) is the highest of any era through 1932** —
1.5× the founding, 1.4× Expansion. Its entire deficit versus the founding is in the *foreign*
component (0.040 vs 0.153, a 3.8× collapse). The founding was fighting Britain and France. The
Civil War president was fighting his own country.

At decade grain the pattern is starker still: the **1860s** foreign component is **0.0220** —
effectively tied with the 1880s (0.0216) for the lowest of the 19th century, and about a twelfth
of the 1810s' 0.265 — while its domestic component is **0.199**, the highest of any decade before
the 1930s. The 1860s is the only decade that is simultaneously at the bottom on foreign
adversaries and at the top on domestic ones.

### 5.2 The adversarial-entity mix confirms it mechanically

Share of adversarial entities typed `nation`:

| 1810s (War of 1812) | 1860s (Civil War) | 1940s (WWII) |
|---|---|---|
| **0.736** | **0.100** | **0.481** |

A **7.4× collapse** in the 1860s. Meanwhile — still at decade grain, matching the table above —
**1860s** `institution` adversaries jump to **0.298** (Congress, the Supreme Court, the House) and
`person` to **0.237**. (The corresponding Civil War *era* figures are 0.165 and 0.242; the
institution share differs by 1.8× between the two grains, which is §5.5's dilution point showing
up again. Where this report quotes a decade figure it says so.)

### 5.3 Who the annotator extracted as Lincoln's adversaries

Across all of Lincoln's corpus paragraphs, the **adversarial entities extracted by the annotation
pass** are **115 group, 89 person, 15 institution — and only 12 nation**. The most frequent are
*"Southern people"* (39), *"Judge Douglas"* (22), *"Senator Douglas"* (21), *"the Judge"* (15),
*"insurgents"* (9), *"the Supreme Court"* (9), *"the rebellion"*, *"seceded States"*.
**"Confederate States" appears as an adversarial entity exactly 3 times.**

**These are derived extractions, not a word count** — the earlier framing of this section as
"Lincoln's own vocabulary" overstated what the field contains. Lincoln's raw text uses
`confeder*` 20 times, `rebel*` 39, `insurgent*` 28 and `seced*` 19; the entity extractor
consolidates and canonicalises those mentions, so the counts above measure *what the annotator
resolved as a named adversary*, not how often Lincoln typed a word. The two are consistent — the
adversary he names is a domestic insurgency, not a sovereign state — but only the first is
evidence about the annotation, which is what this section is arguing about. The historical claim
stands on §5.1 and §5.2, which are rate and composition measures over the whole corpus.

Per-president entity counts ship in `data/combat/by_president.parquet`.

### 5.4 Under genre standardization the gap closes — but this line does not carry weight

Once the genre mix is held fixed, the ordering the bail condition expected appears to reappear:

| Era | `enemy_naming`, genre-standardized | interval | thin-stratum weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Civil War & Recon.** | **0.215** | **none — suppressed** | **0.359** |
| The founding | 0.207 | [0.151, 0.265] | 0.042 |
| Expansion | 0.198 | [0.153, 0.240] | 0.000 |

**Read that table with its third column.** The Civil War standardized cell draws **36% of its
weight from two 3-speech strata** (a `campaign_or_debate` stratum that is three Lincoln campaign
addresses, and a 3-speech `public_remarks_or_address` stratum). Under the thin-stratum rule
(§8.6) that cell gets **no interval at all** — the same treatment `sotu_only` gives the 3
War & New Deal annual messages. So the comparison above is **point estimate versus interval**,
which is not a comparison.

**And the ordering is not robust.** Require every contributing stratum to clear the 5-speech
CI floor and it goes the other way:

| Stratum floor | Civil War | The founding |
|---|---|---|
| 3 speeches (published) | 0.215 *(coverage 0.90)* | 0.207 *(coverage 0.80)* |
| 5 speeches | **0.171** *(coverage 0.58)* | **0.215** *(coverage 0.77)* |

**The ordering reverses.** Neither figure is "the" answer — the 5-speech version buys stability
by dropping a third of the Civil War era's reference coverage, which is why the published
estimator keeps the 3-speech floor for the *point* and disciplines the *interval* instead
(§8.6). But a claim that flips under a defensible change of threshold is not a claim.

**So: this line is withdrawn as evidence.** What survives is the weaker, still-useful statement
that *the founding's raw advantage over the Civil War is substantially a genre-mix effect* — it
does not survive mix control in either direction with any confidence. The adjudication does not
rest on this subsection: §5.1–5.3 carry it, and each is an order-of-magnitude effect rather than
a 0.008 gap between two overlapping point estimates.

### 5.5 The fourth line: era-band dilution

The `trends.ERAS` band 1850–1877 fuses the war decade (1860s: enemy 0.226, party_attack 0.090)
with Reconstruction (1870s: enemy 0.107, party_attack 0.026). **Two-fifths (39.5%, 1,533 of
3,881) of the era's paragraphs are post-1865.** Era aggregation, not annotation, produces part of
the "not elevated" reading.

### 5.6 The flag that *is* a sharp Civil War maximum

`party_attack` in the **1860s is 0.090** — more than double the 1850s (0.037) and the highest of
any decade until the 1930s. The Civil War era *does* show a combativeness spike; it shows it on
the flag that measures domestic adversaries, exactly as a domestic war should.

**Verdict: the bail condition does not fire.** Precisely: **there is no evidence that the
annotation or the era anchoring is broken in the way the bail condition specified.** The
condition's premise conflated "enemy naming" with "conflict intensity," when the flag is
composition-sensitive by construction: a civil war suppresses the foreign-nation component that
dominates the 1810s and 1940s peaks.

That is deliberately narrower than "the annotation and era anchoring are sound". Nothing here
tests **era anchoring** directly — all four falsifiers probe whether the *annotation* mis-fires,
and era anchoring is a property of the rubric (president/party/title masked, decade injected,
judge-against-own-era instruction) that this task consumes rather than validates. Residual
era-bias is disclosed in §8.4 and one of the three designed guards against it (the inter-model
agreement bands) is still missing. "Not broken in the specified way" is what the evidence
supports; "sound" is not.

### 5.7 What would have fired it

Stating this matters, because an adjudication that could not have failed is not an adjudication.
**We would have concluded the annotation or era anchoring was broken if any of the following had
held:**

- The Civil War's **domestic** enemy-naming component had *also* been depressed relative to
  neighbouring eras. It is not — at 0.143 it is the highest of any era through 1932 (§5.1). A
  genuinely broken annotation would have missed adversaries of *both* kinds, not exactly the kind
  the historical situation removed.
- **Per-era entity consistency in the Civil War era had collapsed** — concretely, below ~90%
  against a corpus-wide 96.84%, which would mean its enemy-naming flags were not corroborated by
  the independently-scored entity stance field. It is 97.7%, *above* the corpus mean (§6.1).
- The **adversarial-entity type mix had shown no compositional shift** — i.e. Civil War
  adversaries had still been predominantly `nation`, leaving the low enemy-naming rate
  unexplained. Instead the nation share collapses 7.4× (§5.2).
- **`party_attack` had also been flat** across the 1860s. A war decade showing no elevation on
  *any* combativeness measure would indict the annotation. It more than doubles (§5.6).

All four checks were specified against quantities the data could have returned either way, and
all four came back against the bail condition. Had the evidence pointed the other way, the correct
output was to stop and report a broken annotation — a legitimate result, not a failure.

**Robustness note (cuts in our favour).** **18 of 179** Civil War-era adversarial `nation`
entities are US states or the Confederacy (South Carolina ×8, Virginia ×3, Texas ×2, Wisconsin,
Georgia, Arkansas, North Carolina, Tennessee) — **10.1%**, typed as nations. Reclassifying them
as domestic moves the era's foreign share *down*, **0.206 → 0.186**, **strengthening** the
finding. Computed by `combat.mistyped_nation_recount()` and recorded in `combat_meta.json`, from
the `DOMESTIC_ENTITIES_TYPED_AS_NATION` constant, so the judgment call about which names count is
inspectable rather than buried in a hand tally.

## 6. Cross-checks

### 6.1 Enemy-naming ↔ adversarial-entity consistency, per era

A paragraph flagged `enemy_naming` with no adversarial-stance entity is suspect. Corpus-wide the
rate is **7393/7634 = 96.84%** (matches `notes/annotation-qa-v1.md` exactly). The per-era cut is
this task's contribution — an era where the two independent signals come apart is an era whose
numbers cannot be trusted:

| Era | consistency | n flagged |
|---|---|---|
| The founding | 97.5% | 239 |
| Expansion | 98.3% | 752 |
| Civil War & Recon. | 97.7% | 737 |
| The Gilded Age | 95.9% | 386 |
| Progressives & Depression | 97.6% | 507 |
| War & New Deal | 97.4% | 427 |
| The Cold War | 96.7% | 2156 |
| Post-Cold War | 96.1% | 1041 |
| The present era | 96.2% | 1389 |

**No era collapses.** The full range is 95.9%–98.3%, a 2.4-point spread with no time trend. The
Civil War era sits at 97.7%, *above* the corpus mean — its enemy-naming flags are as well-grounded
as anyone's, which independently rules out "the annotation is broken there."

### 6.2 Lexical baseline (uncorrelated-error cross-check)

The dumb regex proxies from `speech_markers.parquet`, per 10k words, **SOTU-only**:

| Era | `opponents` | `us_them` |
|---|---|---|
| The founding | 0.00 | 71.9 |
| Expansion | 0.34 | 65.6 |
| Civil War & Recon. | 0.47 | 54.3 |
| The Gilded Age | 1.29 | 35.2 |
| Progressives & Depression | 1.89 | 52.1 |
| War & New Deal *(n=3)* | 5.29 | 97.9 |
| The Cold War | 2.69 | 44.2 |
| Post-Cold War | 8.25 | 73.2 |
| **The present era** | **10.64** | **92.9** |

The `opponents` proxy reproduces the `party_attack` finding **without any LLM in the loop**:
present era 10.64 vs Civil War 0.47 — a **22.6×** gap, against the LLM's 4.8× (CI 2.3–14.3).
Direction and ordering agree; the regex gap is larger and sits outside the LLM ratio's interval,
which is expected — `opponents` counts a narrow modern vocabulary that 19th-century prose does not
use even when it is attacking, so it exaggerates the era gap. Agreement here is directional, not
quantitative.

Speech-level Spearman correlations are positive but modest — `party_attack`~`opponents` = 0.45,
`enemy_naming`~`us_them` = 0.25, `zero_sum`~`us_them` = 0.21. That is the *desired* result for an
uncorrelated-error check: the two methods agree on the century-scale trend while failing
independently at the speech level (the regex cannot see sarcasm, euphemism, or an unnamed
"they"). These proxies are corroboration, **never ground truth** — this repo already retracted an
NRC-lexicon tone finding (commit c214a40) for exactly that mistake.

## 7. Receipts

Every point on the chart is paragraph-level, so every point has quotable evidence. Selected from
`data/combat/exemplars.parquet` (ranked by adversarial-entity count — a signal independent of the
flag itself — then by how many of the three flags fired).

**Civil War & Reconstruction — `party_attack`.** Abraham Lincoln, 1858, *"A House Divided"
Speech* (campaign/debate). The conspiracy allegation against Stephen Douglas, Franklin Pierce,
Roger Taney and James Buchanan:

## 7b. Face validity: what the spot-read actually found

The plan specifies spot-reading 10 passages per era per flag. `exemplars.parquet` holds
`10 × 9 eras × 3 flags = 270`. **I read 90 in full — the complete top-10 for all three flags in
the three peak eras (Civil War & Reconstruction, War & New Deal, the present era)**, which are the
cells the headline rests on. The other 180 were not read line-by-line; treat this as a spot-read
of the load-bearing third, not a full audit.

**Result: I would have scored 4 of the 90 differently, and regard 3 more as genuinely arguable.**
That is a ~4% hard-disagreement rate and ~8% including borderline cases.

The four I would score differently:

| Passage | Flag | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Biden 2021, *Address to Joint Session* | `zero_sum` | The paragraph explicitly says the opposite: "But we can also cooperate when it's in our mutual interest." The rubric says arguing *against* division is FALSE. |
| Trump 2019, *Press Conference* (Sept 25) | `zero_sum`, `enemy_naming` | The paragraph is almost entirely the **reporter's question**, not the president's words. Whatever framing exists is the journalist's. |
| Pierce 1856, *Special Session Message* | `zero_sum` | Describes frontier hostilities. The rubric says merely mentioning conflict is not enough. `enemy_naming` here is correct. |
| FDR 1943, *Fireside Chat 24: On the Coal Crisis* | `party_attack` | "the leaders of any one group here back home" targets union leadership — a faction, but not a *partisan* one. |

**Here is one for the reader to disagree with me about.** Joe Biden, 2021, *Address to a Joint
Session of Congress*, scored `zero_sum = TRUE`:

> "And they turned out to be true, so I responded directly and proportionally to Russia's
> interference in our elections and the cyberattacks on our government and our business… **But we
> can also cooperate when it's in our mutual interest. We did it when we extended the New START
> Treaty on nuclear arms, and we're working to do it on climate change.** But he understands we
> will respond."

I read that as a paragraph arguing *against* a purely zero-sum frame, and would score it FALSE.
A reader could reasonably answer that "we will respond" plus "directly and proportionally" is
tit-for-tat framing and the flag is right. The paragraph is in the present era's SOTU cell — the
one carrying the headline — so the disagreement is not cost-free. Judge it yourself; it is row
`(The present era, zero_sum, rank 6)` in `exemplars.parquet`.

**Three findings from the exercise worth recording:**

1. **`zero_sum` is the softest of the three flags.** Three of my four disagreements are `zero_sum`.
   It is also the flag with the weakest lexical corroboration (§6.2) and the one whose present-era
   result is not separable from the founding (§1). Treat `zero_sum` conclusions as the most
   provisional of the three. `party_attack`, which carries the headline, produced one borderline
   disagreement in 30.
2. **The disagreements do not systematically favour the finding.** Two of four are present-era
   passages and two are pre-1945. Correcting all four would nudge the present era's `zero_sum`
   down slightly — i.e. against the direction of the headline, not toward it.
3. **My own first-pass reading was wrong three times.** Reading at 340 characters I initially
   flagged three more passages as misses (Grant 1875 on Louisiana, and two FDR 1940 convention
   passages). Reading them in full reversed all three — Grant's paragraph goes on to accuse the
   Democratic minority of seizing the legislature "by fraud and violence," and FDR's goes on to
   attack "untried hands, inexperienced hands" and "appeaser fifth columnists." **Truncated
   spot-reading produces false disagreements at a meaningful rate**, which is a caution for anyone
   auditing this table and a reason the 4/90 figure should be read as an upper bound on real
   error, not a lower one.

## 8. Limitations — all of them

1. **The 1930s pillar is n=3, and the CI is suppressed.** The War & New Deal era (1933–1945)
   contains only **3 speeches typed as annual messages** (87 paragraphs): FDR's 1941 Four
   Freedoms, the 1943 SOTU, and the 1944 fireside-chat SOTU. The 1930s *decade* SOTU cell is
   also n=3 — and it is **Hoover's** 1930/1931/1932 messages, not FDR's; the Miller Center corpus
   simply does not carry FDR's 1934–1940 annual messages. This is a **corpus-completeness limit,
   not a typing bug** (verified by listing all 55 speeches in 1933–1945).
   *Policy adopted:* a pre-registered n-floor. Below **5** speech clusters no interval is emitted
   at all and the point is marked `suppressed_n_floor`; between 5 and **20** an interval is
   emitted but flagged `low_cluster_caution`. **Do not read the 0.379 / 0.299 War & New Deal SOTU
   numbers as estimates.** The present era's SOTU cell (n=10) carries the caution flag and its
   interval should be read as indicative, though its separation from every other era is far larger
   than the flag's tolerance.

2. **Annotator-disagreement bands are NOT applied.** `inter-model-agreement-check` had not
   produced `data/llm_annotations/agreement_v1.parquet` when this ran. **Every interval in this
   report contains sampling error only** — including the **ratio** intervals, which is the form
   most of the headline numbers take. The seam covers both `combativeness.parquet` and
   `ratios.parquet`: bands widen a ratio interval by scaling each bound by the factor its
   numerator's and denominator's half-widths could move the point ratio, so when the file lands
   the quoted "4.8× [2.3, 14.3]" widens along with the marginals rather than silently standing
   still. It is built, unit-tested, and exercised on every run —
   `combat.load_agreement_bands()`
   returns `None` when the file is absent, and every output row of both tables carries
   `ci_components = "sampling_only"`, `disagreement_band_applied = False`,
   `agreement_source = "absent:agreement_v1.parquet"`. When the file appears, re-running widens
   the bands automatically. **No disagreement magnitude was stubbed, guessed, or hardcoded**, and
   if the file appears without the expected columns the loader raises rather than guessing. Eras
   where Sonnet and Opus disagreed about what counts as combative therefore do *not* yet get
   visibly wider uncertainty. This criterion is **deferred, not met.**

   Three properties of the seam a consumer should know before the file lands:
   * **Ratio widening is violent at small denominators.** A half-width of 0.02 takes the headline
     from `[2.35, 14.32]` to `[1.20, 50.40]`, because the denominator term `den/(den − h)`
     explodes as `h` approaches the Civil War SOTU rate of 0.0296. The qualitative claim survives
     (the lower bound stays above 1), but the *magnitude* would become nearly uninformative. Read
     the ratios as "substantially higher" and nothing finer.
   * **A band applies to a real interval or not at all.** Rows suppressed by either n-floor keep
     `ci_components = "sampling_only"` even when a band exists for their (era, flag) — there is
     no interval for it to widen, and labelling one would assert a component applied to nothing.
     A half-width of exactly 0.0 *does* count as applied: a measured zero is a real component.
   * **The band contract has no treatment dimension.** The key is `(era, flag)`, so one
     half-width is applied identically to `raw`, `sotu_only` and `genre_standardized` — an
     unstated assumption that annotator disagreement does not vary by genre. If
     `inter-model-agreement-check` ships a table keyed by treatment or by model-pair, the loader
     will **raise on duplicate `(era, flag)` keys** rather than silently collapse them. That is
     the right failure mode, but it means the seam may need widening, not just filling.

3. **The 1930s campaign-oratory story is a warning about the raw series, not a footnote.** On the
   raw all-genre chart the 1930s beats the 2020s on `party_attack`. That gap is manufactured by
   **6 campaign speeches** — four from Hoover's 1932 run, two from FDR in 1936 — which carry 85%
   of the decade's flagged paragraphs while occupying three times their reference share of the
   genre mix (§3). Anyone reading the raw series alone would conclude the 1930s presidency was
   more partisan than today's, when what the corpus is actually showing is two election campaigns.
   This is exactly why the raw series is never published alone. Note the corpus's genre coverage is
   uneven in time as well as in kind: it carries Hoover's 1932 campaign speeches but not FDR's
   1934–1940 annual messages (limitation 1), so the decade is sampled at its most combative and
   its least, unevenly.

4. **Residual LLM era-bias is mitigated, not eliminated.** Scoring was era-anchored by
   construction — the rubric (`prompts/annotation_v1.py:302-342`) masks president, party and
   title, injects the speech's decade, and instructs the model to judge against the norms of the
   speech's own era. Three things reduce residual bias: the era-anchored rubric, the entity
   cross-check (§6.1, no era collapses), and the lexical baseline (§6.2, an independent method
   agreeing on direction). But the model's *notion* of "combative" was formed in 2026 and cannot
   be fully de-biased. The plan's mitigation list included the inter-model agreement bands, which
   are missing (limitation 2) — so one of the three designed guards against this risk is absent.

5. **Genre standardization does not have full common support.** No era observes every genre, so
   the reweight runs over the (era, genre) cells with ≥3 speeches and renormalizes. Coverage
   (`ref_weight_covered`) ranges from **0.578** (Expansion, Gilded Age) to **0.996**
   (Progressives). Eras below ~0.8 coverage are standardized over a fraction of the reference mix
   and their standardized rates are not strictly comparable to high-coverage eras.
   **This rule catches the era carrying the headline: the present era covers 0.765 and the 2020s
   decade 0.733**, both under the 0.8 line. What is missing from each is mostly
   `campaign_or_debate` (n=2 in both), the most combative genre in the modern corpus — so the
   omission holds the present era's standardized figure *down*, and the direction is conservative
   for the finding. The SOTU-only series, which is the co-primary and carries the headline, is
   unaffected by coverage entirely.

6. **Thin genre strata used to buy confident intervals; now they suppress them.** The
   standardized estimator averages **within-stratum** resamples, so its interval is only as good
   as its thinnest heavily-weighted stratum — not as good as its total speech count suggests.
   Before this was caught, War & New Deal standardized published `[0.300, 0.439]` with
   `ci_status="ok"` and `n_speeches=52` while drawing **74% of its weight from strata under 5
   speeches — 49% of it from the very 3 annual messages that `sotu_only` suppresses** and that
   §8.1 tells you not to read. The same held for the Civil War era (36%), the 1860s (31%) and the
   1930s (55%).
   *Policy now:* two new columns, `effective_min_cluster` and `weight_from_thin_strata`, are
   published on every row, and `ci_status` is downgraded on a pre-registered rule — thin-stratum
   weight ≥ 0.25 suppresses the interval, ≥ 0.10 flags caution. Four standardized cells lost
   their intervals as a result (Civil War era, War & New Deal era, 1860s, 1930s). **Point
   estimates were kept**: raising the stratum floor to 5 instead would collapse coverage (War &
   New Deal 0.86 → 0.22, Civil War 0.90 → 0.58), trading an interval problem for a worse
   representativeness one.
   The concentration behind the Civil War case is worth naming: its `campaign_or_debate` stratum
   is **three Lincoln speeches, none of them debates** — Peoria (1854), "A House Divided" (1858),
   Cooper Union (1860). A stratum standing in for a tenth of the reference genre mix is one
   author's campaign voice. Prefer SOTU-only wherever the two disagree.

7. **The exemplar ranking surfaces administrative name-lists.** Ranking by adversarial-entity
   count means a proclamation that lists six wanted men outranks a rhetorically sharper paragraph
   naming one. Johnson's 1865 reward proclamation ranks 1st and 3rd for Civil War `enemy_naming`
   partly on that mechanism. The flags are correct; the *ranking* is a convenience, not a
   combativeness score.

8. **This corpus is the formal register only, and that is the finding's force.** No rallies, no
   fundraisers, no social media. This is the room where a knife is *least* likely to come out. The
   corpus's biggest limitation is the result: combativeness has leaked into even this register.
   The flip side is that nothing here measures whether *total* political combativeness rose — only
   that it rose inside the buttoned-up record.

9. **Entity typing has a known quirk.** **10.1%** (18 of 179) of Civil War-era adversarial
   `nation` entities are US states or the Confederacy. Quantified in the robustness note at the
   end of §5.7 and shipped as `combat_meta.json → mistyped_nation_recount`; it moves the result
   in the conservative direction (foreign share 0.206 → 0.186).

10. **The intervals do not cover president-level variation.** The bootstrap clusters on
    **speeches**, but the claim is about *the presidency*, and speeches are nested inside
    administrations. The present era's SOTU cell is 10 speeches across **2 presidents**, and the
    interval treats them as 10 independent draws. Presidents per era's SOTU cell:

    | Era | speeches | presidents |
    |---|---|---|
    | The founding | 27 | 4 |
    | Expansion | 34 | 8 |
    | Civil War & Recon. | 28 | 7 |
    | The Gilded Age | 23 | 5 |
    | Progressives & Depression | 32 | 6 |
    | War & New Deal | 3 | 1 |
    | The Cold War | 34 | 7 |
    | Post-Cold War | 27 | 4 |
    | **The present era** | **10** | **2** |

    Clustering on presidents instead would leave the present era with **2 clusters** — which this
    report's own n-floor would suppress outright. Within-president correlation is never examined
    here, so **the published intervals understate uncertainty about the presidency by an unknown
    amount, and most so in the era carrying the headline.** §4 shows the point estimate is not one
    president's doing; that is a different question from whether 2 presidents pin down an era.
    The gap between the two is the single largest unquantified uncertainty in this report.

11. **Ratios are imprecise even where they are large.** The headline 4.8× carries a 2.3–14.3
    interval; some comparisons are unbounded above (present era ÷ founding on `party_attack` is
    72.8× with CI `[22.5, ∞)`, because the founding's SOTU cell contains a single flagged
    paragraph and 37% of resamples zero the denominator). Every ratio and its interval is in
    `ratios.parquet` with `pct_denominator_zero`. **The defensible claim is "substantially
    higher", not any specific multiple.**

12. **Press-conference paragraphs can contain the interviewer's words.** Found during the
    face-validity read (§7b): at least one present-era exemplar is almost entirely a reporter's
    question, yet carries flags as though the president said it. `press_conference_or_interview`
    is 7.7% of the corpus and 24% of present-era paragraphs, so this could inflate the present
    era's raw and genre-standardized rates by an unmeasured amount. It does **not** affect the
    SOTU-only series that carries the headline. Flagged for follow-up; not quantified here.

13. **An acceptance criterion shipped in a different form than specified, and was not recorded
    as deferred.** The task asked for the three-peaks comparison and the lexical baseline to be
    **plotted**; both ship as markdown tables plus parquets (§3, §6.2). No plotting layer exists
    in this task's Affected Areas — `figures.py` is out of scope here — so tables are defensible,
    but unlike the annotator-band criterion (limitation 2) this substitution was never
    pre-registered as a deferral. Recording it now: **criterion met in substance, not in the
    specified medium.** The parquets are chart-ready.

14. **Two published columns share a name and not a denominator.** `entity_consistency.
    share_foreign_adversary` is the share of *enemy-naming-flagged paragraphs* naming a foreign
    adversary (founding 0.594); `adversary_mix.enemy_foreign` is the share of *all paragraphs*
    (founding 0.153); and `adversary_mix.adv_share_nation` is the share of *adversarial entity
    mentions* (founding 0.592). Three different denominators, similar names. They are documented
    in `adversary_mix`'s docstring; `era-atlas` should read that before joining them.

## 9. Reproduction

```
PYTHONPATH=src arch -x86_64 .venv/bin/python -m presidential_profiles.combat
```

Deterministic: the speech-clustered bootstrap is seeded (`BOOTSTRAP_SEED = 20260721`,
`N_BOOTSTRAP = 2000`), and each cell draws from a generator seeded on
`(seed, group_index, treatment_index)` so results do not depend on iteration order.

**Every one of the ten outputs is byte-identical on rerun, on any date.** None carries a
wall-clock timestamp: a `generated` date in `combat_meta.json` would have dirtied
`git status data/combat/` on every rerun after the generation date, for a value that carries no
information the artifact does not already have. Provenance identity is the `corpus_fingerprint`
(what the numbers were computed against — 1,057 speeches / 36,229 paragraphs /
`23eff144ab94f3fa…`); *when* they landed is git's job.

Outputs in `data/combat/`:

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `combativeness.parquet` | era × flag × treatment × rate × CI × n *(the deliverable)* |
| `peak_decades.parquet` | the same, for 1860s / 1930s / 2020s |
| `ratios.parquet` | present era ÷ every other era, per flag per treatment, with bootstrap CI |
| `exemplars.parquet` | top 10 passages per era per flag, with attribution |
| `entity_consistency.parquet` | per-era enemy-naming ↔ adversarial-entity consistency |
| `lexical_baseline.parquet` | `us_them` / `opponents` per 10k words, raw and SOTU-only |
| `adversary_mix.parquet` | foreign/domestic decomposition + adversarial entity-type shares (§5.2), era and decade grain |
| `by_president.parquet` | per-president flag rates, all-genre and SOTU-only, + adversarial entity counts (§4, §5.3) |
| `genre_decomposition.parquet` | where each era's/decade's flagged paragraphs live, by genre — the `share_of_flagged` column §3 rests on |
| `combat_meta.json` | seed, thresholds, reference weights, corpus fingerprint, agreement status |

Inputs are read-only: `data/llm_annotations/{paragraph_annotations, speech_annotations,
paragraph_entities}.parquet`, `data/{paragraphs, speeches, speech_markers}.parquet`. Era scheme is
`trends.ERAS`, reused unchanged. Corpus fingerprint at time of writing: 1,057 speeches / 36,229
paragraphs / `23eff144ab94f3fa…`.

**Sanity anchors verified against the frozen artifacts:** corpus-wide flag rates `party_attack`
6.56% / `enemy_naming` 21.07% / `zero_sum` 7.68%; SOTU-only `party_attack` present era 0.142,
Civil War 0.030, War & New Deal 0.046; consistency 7393/7634. All reproduce exactly.
